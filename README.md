## The Business Logic & Methodology: Why Seller Central Shows a Profit While Your Bank Account Shrinks

Many new Amazon FBA sellers fall into the same financial trap: the Amazon Seller Central app shows $50,000 in monthly sales, yet there is no cash left in the bank account to pay for the next inventory reorder.

This happens because standard marketplace dashboards only track **platform transactions**. They have no visibility into your supply chain, freight forwarder bills, or changing factory prices. This toolkit is built around four practical accounting realities to close those blind spots for beginners and growing brands alike:

### 1. Factory Price Is Never Your True Product Cost (The Landed Cost Blind Spot)
If you buy a garlic press on Alibaba for **$8.00 per unit**, your cost to sell that unit is much higher than $8.00. Before that item reaches an Amazon FBA fulfillment center, you pay for custom color boxes ($0.40), FNSKU barcode labeling ($0.20), US or UK import customs tariffs ($0.65), and ocean or air freight ($1.75). Your **True Landed Cost (COGS)** is actually **$11.00**. Evaluating your profit margin using the $8.00 factory price inflates your perceived profit by $3.00 on every single unit sold.

### 2. Shared Freight Bills Distort Individual SKU Margins (The CBM & Weight Blind Spot)
When your freight forwarder sends a $3,000 shipping invoice for a single sea shipment containing 500 oversized yoga mats and 2,000 tiny resistance bands, dividing the shipping cost equally per unit destroys your cost accuracy. Small, light items end up subsidizing heavy, bulky items. This spreadsheet allocates shared freight bills across SKUs by **cubic meter volume (CBM)**, **chargeable weight**, or **unit count**, showing you the exact shipping burden each product carries.

### 3. Supplier Price Increases Ruin Historical Profit Tracking (The Batch COGS Blind Spot)
Suppose you bought Batch #1 in January at a landed cost of $8.00 per unit, and raw material inflation pushed Batch #2 in March up to $10.00 per unit. In a basic spreadsheet, changing the SKU cost cell from $8.00 to $10.00 overwrites your January and February profit reports, making it look like your business was less profitable all year. Our **Batch-Level COGS Engine** matches historical sales to the exact procurement batch active during that reporting period.

### 4. Gross Margin Hides Platform Fee Bleed (The Net Operating Profit Blind Spot)
A product bringing in $100.00 in net sales with $35.00 in landed COGS appears to have a healthy $65.00 gross profit. However, once Amazon deducts $15.00 in referral commissions, $10.00 in FBA pick-and-pack fulfillment fees, $20.00 in Sponsored Products PPC advertising spend, and $8.00 in monthly warehouse storage and return processing fees, your **True Net Operating Profit** is only **$12.00 (12% net margin)**. By bringing supply chain costs and overlapping Seller Central expense reports into one table, you see exactly how many dollars remain after every cost of doing business is paid.

### Decision Framework

The toolkit is built around five connected accounting and management decisions.

#### 1. What Does Each Product Actually Cost?

The first decision is to establish the complete procurement cost of each product rather than relying on the factory invoice price.

The landed cost calculation includes:

- Alibaba manufacturing cost.
- Custom packaging cost.
- Import duties.
- FBA preparation charges.
- Allocated international freight and customs-related logistics expenses.

The resulting unit landed cost becomes the cost basis for subsequent profitability analysis.

#### 2. Which Procurement Cost Should Be Applied to Historical Sales?

The workbook supports two alternative COGS methods:

| Method | Calculation Logic | Appropriate Use |
|---|---|---|
| Historical Cumulative Weighted Average | Uses all qualifying procurement batches received on or before the reporting month to calculate a weighted average landed cost. | Evaluating historical profitability across changing procurement prices. |
| Latest Batch Standard Cost | Uses the latest recorded unit landed cost for the corresponding Master SKU. | Evaluating profitability against the most recent procurement cost baseline. |

The historical weighted-average method respects the procurement timeline.

A purchase batch received in March must not automatically affect the calculated COGS of January sales.

The method is controlled centrally and can be switched without rebuilding the calculation engine.

#### 3. How Should Shared International Freight Be Allocated?

International freight invoices often cover multiple products in a consolidated shipment.

The workbook supports three allocation bases:

| Allocation Method | Allocation Basis | Typical Application |
|---|---|---|
| Volume-based | Product shipment CBM | Sea freight and volume-sensitive logistics |
| Weight-based | Chargeable KG | Air freight and weight-sensitive logistics |
| Quantity-based | Received units | Situations where unit-based allocation is commercially appropriate |

The default allocation rule is controlled centrally.

Individual shipment records can override the global rule when a particular shipment requires different allocation treatment.

#### 4. Which Amazon Expenses Should Actually Be Included?

Amazon expense data can overlap across settlement, advertising, and storage reports.

The workbook uses two conditions to determine whether an expense record is eligible for inclusion:

1. Its unique expense ID must appear only once.
2. Its original report source must match the designated authoritative report for that expense category.

The result is a standardized expense record that can be assigned to the correct SKU, marketplace, and reporting month.

#### 5. Is the Product Generating Acceptable Operating Profit?

Revenue and gross margin alone are insufficient for evaluating Amazon SKU performance.

The final decision framework considers:

- Net sales revenue after customer refunds.
- Historical landed COGS.
- Amazon referral commissions and fulfillment expenses.
- Advertising expenditure.
- Storage and miscellaneous platform expenses.
- Gross profit and operating profit.
- Profitability thresholds and procurement cost changes.

This makes it possible to distinguish products with attractive gross margins from products that actually generate acceptable operating profitability.

### Three Traps That Catch Even Experienced Amazon Sellers

#### Trap 1: Using Today's Purchase Price to Evaluate Yesterday's Sales

**The decision:** Management reviews January profitability and concludes that the product has become significantly less profitable.

**The unnoticed assumption:** The latest supplier purchase price is treated as the cost of every unit sold, regardless of when the inventory was received.

Consider a product with the following procurement history:

| Procurement Batch | Receipt Month | Quantity | Unit Landed Cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Batch A | January | 500 | CA$10.00 |
| Batch B | March | 500 | CA$13.00 |

If 100 units were sold in January, applying the March cost would produce:

| Calculation | Correct Historical Cost | Incorrect Latest-Cost Approach |
|---|---:|---:|
| Units sold | 100 | 100 |
| Unit cost | CA$10.00 | CA$13.00 |
| COGS | CA$1,000 | CA$1,300 |

The incorrect method overstates January COGS by CA$300.

It creates a false impression that historical profitability was worse than it actually was.

**Corrected approach:** Match procurement costs against the reporting-month cutoff. Only batches received on or before the reporting month participate in the historical weighted-average calculation.

<details>
<summary>Formula Reference — Historical Procurement Cost Matching</summary>

```excel
=LET(
    HistCostSum,
        SUMIFS(
            '03_Procurement_Landed_Cost'!P6#,
            '03_Procurement_Landed_Cost'!D6:D5000,E6#,
            '03_Procurement_Landed_Cost'!C6:C5000,"<="&B6#
        ),
    HistQtySum,
        SUMIFS(
            '03_Procurement_Landed_Cost'!F6:F5000,
            '03_Procurement_Landed_Cost'!D6:D5000,E6#,
            '03_Procurement_Landed_Cost'!C6:C5000,"<="&B6#
        ),
    IF(HistQtySum>0,HistCostSum/HistQtySum,0)
)
```

The production implementation also includes the configurable COGS method and latest-cost fallback.

</details>

**Decision outcome:** Historical profitability is evaluated using the cost history relevant to the period, while recent purchasing cost increases remain visible as a separate management warning.

#### Trap 2: Counting the Same Amazon Expense More Than Once

**The decision:** Management observes that operating profit has deteriorated and decides to reduce advertising expenditure.

**The unnoticed assumption:** Every expense record exported from Amazon represents a separate economic expense.

For example, the same advertising expenditure may appear in a settlement-related report and a dedicated Sponsored Ads report.

If both are imported and accumulated without source controls, the expense can be counted twice.

Illustrative example:

| Expense Source | Advertising Amount |
|---|---:|
| Settlement report | CA$1,000 |
| Sponsored Ads report | CA$1,000 |
| Incorrect total | CA$2,000 |
| Correct authoritative expense | CA$1,000 |

The apparent CA$1,000 additional expense is a reporting duplication, not an actual increase in advertising expenditure.

Reducing advertising based on this distorted figure could damage sales without solving the real problem.

**Corrected approach:** Assign one authoritative source to each expense category and require each expense record to have a unique identifier.

<details>
<summary>Formula Reference — Expense Source and Duplicate Filtering</summary>

```excel
=LET(
    RecID,FILTER(A6:A50000,A6:A50000<>"",""),
    ReportSrc,FILTER(E6:E50000,A6:A50000<>"",""),
    FeeCat,J6#,
    SourceMatch,
        IFS(
            FeeCat="平台佣金与配送费",
                ReportSrc='01_Control_Panel'!$C$9,
            FeeCat="广告推广费",
                ReportSrc='01_Control_Panel'!$C$10,
            FeeCat="仓储与杂项费",
                ReportSrc='01_Control_Panel'!$C$11,
            TRUE,FALSE
        ),
    UniqueCheck,COUNTIF(A6:A50000,RecID)=1,
    --(SourceMatch*UniqueCheck)
)
```

The effective coefficient is:

- `1`: Authoritative source and unique expense ID.
- `0`: Duplicate ID or non-authoritative source.

The coefficient is subsequently multiplied by the original expense amount and applicable exchange rate.

</details>

**Decision outcome:** Operating profitability reflects the accepted expense records rather than the number of reports in which the same transaction appears.

#### Trap 3: Treating Gross Margin as the Final Profitability Measure

**The decision:** Management continues promoting a product because its gross margin appears attractive.

**The unnoticed assumption:** Revenue minus product COGS represents the amount available as operating profit.

Consider a SKU with the following monthly performance:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net sales revenue | CA$5,000 |
| Landed COGS | CA$2,000 |
| Gross profit | CA$3,000 |
| Amazon commissions and fulfillment | CA$1,200 |
| Advertising expenditure | CA$1,000 |
| Storage and miscellaneous expenses | CA$500 |
| Operating profit | CA$300 |

The product has a 60% gross margin but only a 6% operating margin.

The difference is commercially significant.

A product that looks attractive at the procurement and selling-price level may deliver a much weaker return after the costs of operating on Amazon are included.

**Corrected approach:** Evaluate gross profitability and operating profitability separately.

The gross margin measures the relationship between revenue and landed COGS. The operating margin reflects the additional Amazon expenses included in this toolkit.

**Decision outcome:** Management can distinguish between products that deserve additional advertising investment, products requiring cost optimization, and products whose operating economics need to be reconsidered.

### Example Scenario

**Scenario: Evaluating a product sold across multiple Amazon marketplaces**

A business sources products from Alibaba and distributes inventory across Amazon US, Canada, UK, and Australia.

The management team wants to understand whether its core product remains profitable after accounting for procurement, freight, foreign exchange, and Amazon operating expenses.

The following example uses illustrative figures to demonstrate the calculation process. All amounts are expressed in CAD after currency conversion.

#### Step 1. Establish Procurement Costs

A shipment contains a product with the following procurement records:

| Cost Component | Original Amount |
|---|---:|
| Manufacturing: 100 units × US$8.00 | US$800 |
| Custom packaging: 100 units × US$0.50 | US$50 |
| Import duties | US$120 |
| FBA preparation | US$80 |
| Total direct procurement and preparation | US$1,050 |

Assume the applicable USD-to-CAD exchange rate is 1.35.

Direct procurement and preparation costs:

`US$1,050 × 1.35 = CA$1,417.50`

The shipment also incurs US$400 in shared international freight.

For this simplified example, assume the product represents the entire allocation base of the shipment.

Allocated freight:

`US$400 × 1.35 = CA$540.00`

Total landed cost:

`CA$1,417.50 + CA$540.00 = CA$1,957.50`

Unit landed cost:

`CA$1,957.50 ÷ 100 = CA$19.575`

#### Step 2. Consolidate Marketplace Sales

Assume the product generates the following monthly net sales after customer refunds:

| Marketplace | Net Revenue (CAD) |
|---|---:|
| Amazon US | CA$2,000 |
| Amazon CA | CA$1,200 |
| Amazon UK | CA$1,300 |
| Amazon AU | CA$900 |
| **Total** | **CA$5,400** |

The different marketplace settlement currencies are converted into the group's CAD reporting currency before consolidation.

#### Step 3. Match Historical COGS

Assume 50 net units were sold during the reporting month and the applicable historical weighted-average landed cost is CA$19.575 per unit.

Total COGS:

`50 × CA$19.575 = CA$978.75`

Gross profit:

`CA$5,400 − CA$978.75 = CA$4,421.25`

#### Step 4. Deduct Amazon Operating Expenses

| Expense Category | Amount (CAD) |
|---|---:|
| Commissions and fulfillment | CA$1,600 |
| Advertising | CA$900 |
| Storage and miscellaneous | CA$250 |
| **Total Operating Expenses** | **CA$2,750** |

Operating profit:

`CA$4,421.25 − CA$2,750 = CA$1,671.25`

Operating margin:

`CA$1,671.25 ÷ CA$5,400 = 30.95%`

#### Step 5. Interpret the Result

The product generates CA$5,400 in net sales and CA$1,671.25 in operating profit under the toolkit's defined cost scope.

However, the consolidated result is only the starting point for management analysis.

The next questions are:

- Which marketplace produces the highest operating margin?
- Is advertising expenditure disproportionately high in a particular marketplace?
- Is the latest procurement batch more expensive than the historical weighted average?
- Does the product meet the target operating margin?
- Would changing the freight allocation method materially affect its reported profitability?

The dashboard provides the consolidated view, while the underlying calculation engine retains marketplace and SKU-level detail.

**The practical outcome:** Management moves from evaluating sales volume and headline gross margin to examining the actual economics of sourcing, importing, and selling each product across different Amazon marketplaces.

#### 2. Procurement & Landed Cost Calculations

<details>
<summary>03_Procurement_Landed_Cost — Batch-Level Landed Cost Engine</summary>

**A. Shipment volume and weight**

Each procurement batch retrieves the product's physical dimensions from the master data.

```excel
=LET(
    ValidRows,A6:A5000<>"",
    MasterSKU,FILTER(D6:D5000,ValidRows,""),
    Qty,FILTER(F6:F5000,ValidRows,0),
    UnitCBM,
        XLOOKUP(
            MasterSKU,
            '02_Product_Master'!C6:C2000,
            '02_Product_Master'!F6:F2000,
            0
        ),
    Qty*UnitCBM
)
```

The same logic is used for total chargeable weight, retrieving the unit weight from the product master.

**B. Direct procurement cost**

The direct procurement and preparation cost is calculated as:

```excel
=((Manufacturing + Packaging) * Quantity
  + Import Duties
  + Preparation Fees)
  * Applicable FX Rate
```

All amounts are converted into the group reporting currency.

**C. Shared international freight allocation**

The allocation method is selected using either the global default or a shipment-specific override.

For each procurement batch:

```text
Allocated Freight =
Shipment Freight in Reporting Currency
×
Batch Allocation Basis
÷
Total Shipment Allocation Basis
```

The allocation basis can be:

- Total CBM.
- Total chargeable KG.
- Total received units.

The denominator is calculated from all procurement batches associated with the same shipment ID.

**D. Unit landed cost**

```excel
' Batch Total Landed Cost
=N6# + O6#

' Unit Landed Cost
=LET(
    Qty,FILTER(F6:F5000,A6:A5000<>"",0),
    IF(Qty>0,P6#/Qty,0)
)
```

The resulting unit landed cost is stored at procurement-batch level and becomes the cost input for historical COGS matching.

</details>

#### 3. Sales & Expense Standardization

<details>
<summary>04_Amazon_Sales_Data — Net Sales and Refund Treatment</summary>

**Marketplace SKU mapping**

```excel
=LET(
    MarketSKUs,
        FILTER(D6:D50000,A6:A50000<>"",""),
    XLOOKUP(
        MarketSKUs,
        '02_Product_Master'!A6:A2000,
        '02_Product_Master'!C6:C2000,
        "UNMAPPED_SKU"
    )
)
```

**Net sales units**

```excel
=LET(
    ValidRows,A6:A50000<>"",
    FILTER(F6:F50000,ValidRows,0)
    -FILTER(H6:H50000,ValidRows,0)
)
```

**Net sales revenue**

```excel
=LET(
    ValidRows,A6:A50000<>"",
    FILTER(G6:G50000,ValidRows,0)
    -FILTER(I6:I50000,ValidRows,0)
)
```

Customer refund amounts are treated as reductions in product revenue.

They are not automatically treated as additional operating expenses.

**Reporting currency conversion**

```excel
=Net Revenue in Local Currency
 * Applicable Monthly FX Rate
```

When the transaction currency equals the group reporting currency, the applicable rate is 1.0000.

</details>

<details>
<summary>05_Amazon_Expense_Data — Expense Classification and Duplicate Filtering</summary>

**Standard expense classification**

The workbook consolidates detailed Amazon expense types into three management accounting categories:

| Standard Category | Representative Original Expense Types |
|---|---|
| Platform Commissions & Fulfillment | Referral Fee, FBA Fulfillment Fee, Refund Commission |
| Advertising | PPC Spend, Sponsored Brands, Advertising |
| Storage & Miscellaneous | Monthly Storage Fee, Disposal Fee, and other miscellaneous platform expenses |

**Duplicate filtering**

Two conditions determine whether an expense record is accepted:

1. Its unique expense ID must occur exactly once.
2. Its report source must match the authoritative source configured for its expense category.

The resulting coefficient is multiplied by the original expense amount and applicable FX rate.

```excel
=Raw Expense Amount
 * Expense Validity Coefficient
 * Applicable FX Rate
```

A rejected record receives an effective coefficient of zero.

**Important implementation consideration**

Expense classification depends on the original Amazon expense type names and the configured authoritative report-source identifiers.

New expense categories or changes in report naming should be reviewed before importing them into the production workbook.

</details>

```excel
    ValidFX,B6:B500<>"",

```

#### 5. Historical COGS & Profitability Engine

<details>
<summary>07_COGS_Profit_Engine — Historical Cost Matching and SKU Profitability</summary>

This worksheet is a formula-only calculation engine. It has no manually maintained input columns.

**A. Generate the monthly marketplace SKU combinations**

The engine extracts unique combinations from sales records within the configured reporting period.

```excel
=LET(
    SalesMonth,'04_Amazon_Sales_Data'!B6:B50000,
    SalesMkt,'04_Amazon_Sales_Data'!C6:C50000,
    SalesSKU,'04_Amazon_Sales_Data'!D6:D50000,
    StartM,'01_Control_Panel'!$C$3,
    EndM,'01_Control_Panel'!$C$4,
    ValidFilter,
        (SalesMonth<>"")
        *(SalesMonth>=StartM)
        *(SalesMonth<=EndM),
    RawKeys,
        FILTER(
            SalesMonth&"|"&SalesMkt&"|"&SalesSKU,
            ValidFilter,
            "无满足条件数据"
        ),
    SORT(UNIQUE(RawKeys))
)
```

This generates the calculation grain:

`Month × Marketplace × Marketplace SKU`

**B. Historical weighted-average landed cost**

For a given Master SKU and reporting month:

```text
Historical Weighted Average Cost =
Total Landed Cost of Eligible Procurement Batches
÷
Total Eligible Received Quantity
```

Only procurement batches with receipt months on or before the reporting month are included.

**C. Latest batch standard cost**

The alternative method retrieves the latest recorded unit landed cost for the corresponding Master SKU.

The selected COGS method is controlled through `01_Control_Panel!C8`.

**D. Total COGS**

```excel
=Net Sales Units * Matched Historical Unit Landed Cost
```

**E. Gross profit and gross margin**

```excel
' Gross Profit
=Net Sales Revenue - Total COGS

' Gross Margin
=IF(Net Sales Revenue>0,
    Gross Profit/Net Sales Revenue,
    0
)
```

**F. Operating expenses**

The engine aggregates three expense categories by reporting month, marketplace, and marketplace SKU:

- Platform commissions and fulfillment.
- Advertising.
- Storage and miscellaneous expenses.

Only expense records with an effective validity coefficient are included.

**G. Operating profit and operating margin**

```excel
' Operating Profit
=Gross Profit
 - Platform Commissions and Fulfillment
 - Advertising
 - Storage and Miscellaneous Expenses

' Operating Margin
=IF(Net Sales Revenue>0,
    Operating Profit/Net Sales Revenue,
    0
)
```

**H. Profitability diagnostic labels**

The engine evaluates each SKU using the following priority:

| Condition | Diagnostic Label |
|---|---|
| Marketplace SKU is not mapped | Unmapped SKU |
| Historical landed cost is missing | Missing COGS |
| Operating profit is negative | Severe Operating Loss |
| Gross margin is below the minimum threshold | Low Gross Margin |
| Latest cost deviates beyond the configured threshold | Procurement Cost Change |
| Operating margin meets or exceeds the target | High-Performing SKU |
| Other qualifying records | Normal Profitability |

The order of evaluation matters. A product with missing COGS should not be treated as a healthy, highly profitable product merely because its calculated COGS is zero.

</details>

#### Important Calculation Guard Conditions

The documented implementation uses several defensive calculation rules:

- Reporting-currency transactions use an exchange rate of `1.0000`.
- Missing exchange-rate lookups return `0` in the documented lookup formulas.
- Unit landed cost returns `0` when received quantity is zero.
- Gross and operating margin return `0` when net revenue is not positive.
- Missing Master SKU mappings return `UNMAPPED_SKU`.
- Duplicate or non-authoritative expense records receive a validity coefficient of `0`.
- Missing historical procurement cost is identified separately rather than interpreted as genuine zero-cost inventory.

**Data integrity limitation:** A zero exchange-rate fallback can silently produce zero-valued converted transactions if the missing rate is not investigated. The validation workflow must therefore be completed before the profitability dashboard is treated as reliable.

**Implementation verification:** The specification describes orphan SKU monitoring across sales and expense records, while its documented `F6` example explicitly filters the sales table. Expense-side orphan SKU coverage should be verified during workbook implementation rather than assumed from the field description alone.

#### Dynamic Array Integrity

The workbook depends on modern Excel dynamic-array calculations.

The following rules apply:

1. Formula-generated areas must remain free of manually entered values.
2. New records should be appended within the designated input ranges.
3. Formula cells must not be overwritten with static values.
4. Dynamic spill ranges must remain unobstructed.
5. Excel calculation mode must remain set to Automatic.
6. Formula results should be reviewed after changes to accounting parameters or imported records.

A `#SPILL!` error generally indicates that another cell is blocking the intended output range.

The obstruction should be removed without deleting the original formula in the spill's starting cell.

## The Business Logic & Methodology

This toolkit combines several management accounting methods to address a central business question: **Which products actually generate acceptable profit after the complete costs of sourcing, importing, and selling across different Amazon marketplaces are considered?**

Rather than treating sales, procurement, logistics, and platform expenses as independent reporting activities, the methodology connects them into one product-level profitability framework.

- **Landed Cost Accounting:** Manufacturing, packaging, import duties, preparation, and shared freight are combined to establish the actual cost of inventory entering the fulfillment process. This prevents factory purchase prices from understating product costs and distorting pricing decisions.

- **Weighted-Average Historical Costing:** Procurement batches are evaluated according to their receipt history. This prevents recent supplier price increases from retrospectively changing the cost basis of earlier sales and makes historical margin analysis more meaningful.

- **Activity-Based Freight Allocation:** Shared international freight is distributed using product volume, chargeable weight, or received quantity. This provides a more commercially meaningful view of each product's logistics burden than simply dividing a shipment invoice equally among products.

- **Multi-Currency Consolidation:** Sales, procurement, and expenses are translated into one group reporting currency using the applicable monthly exchange rates. This makes marketplace comparisons possible without confusing currency differences with genuine operating performance.

- **Expense Reconciliation and Profitability Analysis:** Unique transaction identifiers and authoritative report-source rules prevent overlapping platform expenses from inflating costs. Gross margin and operating margin are then evaluated separately to distinguish product economics from the additional costs of selling through Amazon.

Together, these methods turn fragmented operational records into a consistent basis for evaluating marketplace performance, identifying underperforming products, monitoring supplier cost changes, and prioritizing pricing or cost-reduction decisions.
